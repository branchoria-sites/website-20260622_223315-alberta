## Introduction

Does Arctic radar make UFO reports stronger? In some cases, yes—but not in the simple way many people assume. [Nunavut]({{ 'what-really-shows-up-over-nunavut/' | relative_url }}) lies beneath part of North America’s northern surveillance network, including the North Warning System (NWS), a chain of radar stations operated for continental air defence. If an unusual object is detected by radar as well as seen by witnesses, investigators generally regard that as more significant than a visual report alone. However, radar does not provide automatic proof that a mysterious object was a solid craft, let alone something extraordinary. The North Warning System was designed to warn of potential airborne threats approaching North America, not to act as a public UFO-investigation service. Its data are often classified, incomplete from a public perspective, and subject to technical limitations that can create uncertainty rather than eliminate it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/operations/allies-partners/norad.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada+2Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD)1 Apr 2026 — The North is a critical region for NORAD. ## What the North Warning System watches for

The North Warning System replaced the Cold War-era DEW Line and remains a key part of NORAD’s northern aerospace surveillance network. It consists of long-range and short-range radar sites stretching across the Arctic from Alaska through northern Canada to Greenland. Nunavut contains several of these facilities and sits within the broader surveillance zone that watches northern approaches to the continent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/operations/allies-partners/norad.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada+2Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD)1 Apr 2026 — The North is a critical region for NORAD. Its purpose is straightforward: detect and track aircraft, cruise missiles and other potential airborne threats approaching North America. Radar information is fed into NORAD’s command structure, where it is combined with data from other sensors and monitoring systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasittuq.com/projects/north-warning-system/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasittuq.com">[Nasittuq Corporation+2CGAI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasittuq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">north warning system</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nasittuq CorporationNORTH WARNING SYSTEMIt detects aircraft and cruise missiles flying within its radar coverage, and spans over 5000 kil...</span></span></span>

This mission shapes how UFO-related claims should be interpreted. The system was built to answer a defence question—"Is something entering monitored [airspace]({{ 'airspace/' | relative_url }}) that could threaten North America?"—rather than a UFO question such as "What exactly was that light seen over a remote community?" A radar network can detect movement and position, but it does not automatically reveal an object's identity, origin or nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navalassoc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Gilmour-NORAD-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navalassoc.ca">[Naval Association of Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navalassoc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval Association of Canada NORAD: Renewal of the North Warning System by Canada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Association of CanadaNORAD: Renewal of the North Warning System by CanadaAugust 6, 2021 — by JG Gilmour · Cited by 5 — NORAD was de...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 6, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why radar records are not public UFO answers

One of the most common assumptions in UFO discussions is that military radar must contain definitive answers. In reality, the public rarely sees the full picture.

Most North Warning System data are collected for operational defence purposes. Radar tracks, sensor performance details and investigation results are not routinely released in a form that allows civilian researchers to reconstruct an event. Even when an unusual detection occurs, public reporting may be limited to brief official statements or later summaries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/news/2022/01/backgrounder--north-warning-system-in-service-support.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">backgrounder north warning system in service support</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Backgrounder – North Warning System In-Service Support31 Jan 2022 — This new contract will ensure that NWS radar data is available...</span></span></span>

This creates a gap between public expectations and available evidence. A witness might believe that "the radar must know what it was", while outside observers have no access to the underlying data. In many cases, the public cannot independently determine whether a radar contact represented an aircraft, atmospheric effect, equipment anomaly, balloon, or something else entirely.

The problem is not unique to Nunavut. Across North America, military and aviation officials have repeatedly noted that unidentified radar tracks remain exactly that: unidentified. A radar contact may indicate something worth investigating, but the existence of a track alone does not establish what produced it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/en/article/canada-military-rcaf-ufos-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">canada military rcaf ufos sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Credible UFO Reports Are Being Ignored, Declassified...29 Nov 2021 — “It is important to remember that unknown tracks on radar are p...</span></span></span>

## How surveillance can both help and mislead

Radar evidence is valuable, but it has limits that are often overlooked in popular UFO discussions.

A radar return can strengthen a case when several forms of evidence point in the same direction. For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Witnesses report an object.
* Pilots observe the same object.
* Radar systems detect a corresponding track.
* Multiple sensors agree on its location and movement.

</div>

In such situations, investigators gain more confidence that something physical was present.

Yet radar can also introduce confusion. Modern radar systems must constantly distinguish meaningful targets from weather effects, birds, atmospheric conditions, signal reflections and other forms of clutter. Unknown returns sometimes disappear after further analysis or are reclassified once additional information becomes available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/en/article/canada-military-rcaf-ufos-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">canada military rcaf ufos sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Credible UFO Reports Are Being Ignored, Declassified...29 Nov 2021 — “It is important to remember that unknown tracks on radar are p...</span></span></span>

The 2023 North American airspace incidents provided a useful public example of this problem. After heightened surveillance following the Chinese surveillance balloon episode, NORAD adjusted radar filters and detection settings to better identify smaller objects. Officials subsequently acknowledged that changing how sensors searched for targets affected what operators were seeing. In other words, detection systems do not simply reveal reality unchanged; they also depend on how the systems are configured to interpret signals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://defensescoop.com/2023/02/13/norad-adjusts-radar-gates-to-sharpen-detection-of-anomalous-objects-as-ufo-recovery-intensifies/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defensescoop.com">[DefenseScoop]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defensescoop.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NORAD adjusts radar &#x27;gates&#x27; to sharpen detection of anomalous objects as UFO recovery intensifies.Read more...</span></span></span>

For UFO interpretation, that is an important caution. A newly detected object may represent a genuine target, but an increase in detections can also reflect changes in surveillance methods rather than a sudden increase in unusual phenomena.

## Why even Arctic radar has blind spots

Another misconception is that northern radar provides complete visibility across the Arctic.

Defence analysts and Canadian policy studies have long noted limitations in the current North Warning System. The network was designed primarily to detect particular categories of airborne threats and does not provide perfect coverage of every altitude, direction or type of object. Researchers discussing [NORAD modernisation]({{ 'new-sensors/' | relative_url }}) have highlighted concerns about gaps in coverage, challenges posed by the Earth's curvature, and difficulties in tracking newer forms of low-altitude or unconventional threats.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366896984_The_North_Warning_System_and_Canada-The_Case_for_Renewal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2Queen&#x27;s University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) The North Warning System and Canada-The Case...Another problem with the NWS is that the current range of the radar sys...</span></span></span>

Even official discussions about replacing or modernising the system acknowledge that the existing network is ageing and was built for a different strategic era. War on the Rocks+2The Simons Foundation<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://warontherocks.com/beyond-the-north-warning-system/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: warontherocks.com">[warontherocks.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">warontherocks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">beyond the north warning system</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">War on the RocksBeyond the North Warning System7 Sept 2020 — Upgrading the North Warning System exclusively in a NORAD context is not suf...</span></span></span>

For UFO researchers, this means two things that may seem contradictory but are both true:

* A lack of radar evidence does not necessarily prove that nothing was present.
* A radar detection does not automatically reveal what was present.

Both overconfidence and excessive scepticism can therefore misread the evidence.

## What this means for Nunavut UFO reports

Nunavut's vast geography, sparse population and strategic location make military monitoring an important part of the territory's UFO context. Reports involving pilots, aircraft routes or northern defence infrastructure naturally attract more attention than isolated visual sightings because there is at least a possibility that additional sensor data exist somewhere within aviation or military systems.

However, the mere presence of the North Warning System should not be treated as a shortcut to certainty.
