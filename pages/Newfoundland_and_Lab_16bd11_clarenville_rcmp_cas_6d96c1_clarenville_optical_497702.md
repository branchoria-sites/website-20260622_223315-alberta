## Introduction

The reported use of binoculars and a high-powered scope is one of the features that makes the 1978 [Clarenville]({{ 'clarenville/' | relative_url }}) sighting stand out in [Newfoundland and Labrador]({{ 'newfoundland-and-labrador/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history. Unlike many reports that involve a distant light glimpsed briefly with the naked eye, witnesses in Clarenville claimed they spent a prolonged period examining the object through magnifying optical equipment. That detail has often been cited as strengthening the testimony. At the same time, optical aids have important limits: they can reveal more visible detail, but they cannot by themselves determine an object's distance, size, speed, or identity. ## What they cannot prove about distance or identity

The strongest caution in evaluating the Clarenville optical evidence is that magnification does not solve the problem of distance.

A bright object seen at night can appear surprisingly detailed through binoculars while still being many kilometres away. Without an independently known range, observers cannot accurately calculate an object's true size, altitude, or speed. A small nearby object and a large distant object can produce similar visual impressions. This is a well-known limitation in visual observation and one reason aviation and astronomical investigations rely on multiple forms of evidence rather than magnified viewing alone.

Atmospheric conditions create another challenge. Light travelling over water at night can be affected by haze, temperature layers, moisture, and optical distortion. Magnification enlarges the image, but it can also enlarge distortions. Apparent edges, shapes, and textures may not correspond perfectly to the actual source. A scope can therefore increase perceived detail without guaranteeing that every observed feature is physically real.

The same limitation applies to descriptions of surface texture. In later recollections, Blackwood described the object as having a dull, coarse appearance rather than the shiny finish he associated with conventional aircraft. Such impressions are intriguing, but they remain subjective visual observations made under night-time conditions and cannot be independently verified. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://canadiancoinnews.com/rcms-third-ufo-coin-features-1978-clarenville-event/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadiancoinnews.com">[Canadian Coin News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadiancoinnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canadian Coin NewsRCM&#x27;s third UFO coin features 1978 &#x27;Clarenville Event&#x27;8 Oct 2020 — Using binoculars and a high-powered scope, they obse...</span></span></span>
