## Introduction

The University of [Manitoba]({{ 'why-manitoba-matters-in-canadian-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s UFO archive matters because it changes UFO claims from stories that circulate in books, documentaries and media reports into records that can be examined by anyone willing to do the work. Within Manitoba’s place in Canada’s UFO history, the archive is significant not because it proves extraterrestrial visitation, but because it preserves documents, photographs, correspondence, artefacts and investigative files that can be checked, compared and challenged. That shift—from private collection to public archive—is one of the most important developments in Canadian UFO research. When materials are held by a university archive rather than by individual investigators, future researchers can inspect the evidence directly instead of relying on second-hand summaries. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://universityaffairs.ca/news/the-university-of-manitobas-archive-of-the-paranormal-just-became-a-little-more-extraordinary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universityaffairs.ca">[University Affairs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universityaffairs.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University AffairsThe University of Manitoba&#x27;s archive of the paranormal just...Feb 25, 2020 — The donation includes 20,000 UFO reports...</span></span></span>

## Why Put UFO Files in a University Archive?

Universities routinely preserve political papers, scientific records and community history. Preserving UFO material follows the same archival logic. The question is not whether every claim is true; it is whether the records have historical, cultural or research value.

The University of Manitoba accepted Rutkowski’s collection as part of its [Archives]({{ 'archives/' | relative_url }}) and Special Collections programme. By doing so, the university created a setting where unusual claims could be preserved without endorsing them. Researchers can inspect original documents, compare versions of events, trace how stories changed over time and assess the quality of evidence for themselves. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eventscalendar.umanitoba.ca/site/libraries/event/the-falcon-lake-ufo-files/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eventscalendar.umanitoba.ca">[Events Calendar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eventscalendar.umanitoba.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Events Calendar Libraries</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Events CalendarLibraries - “The Falcon Lake UFO Files”Nov 7, 2019 — With this lecture, Chris Rutkowski launches the creation of his UFO A...</span></span></span>

This is especially important in UFO research because many cases accumulate legends over decades. Archival preservation allows investigators to return to original witness statements, field notes and correspondence rather than relying on later retellings. In practical terms, an archive often strengthens scrutiny rather than belief. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://universityaffairs.ca/news/the-university-of-manitobas-archive-of-the-paranormal-just-became-a-little-more-extraordinary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universityaffairs.ca">[University Affairs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universityaffairs.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University AffairsThe University of Manitoba&#x27;s archive of the paranormal just...Feb 25, 2020 — The donation includes 20,000 UFO reports...</span></span></span>

## What Rutkowski Donated to the University

The collection announced in 2019 was unusually large by Canadian standards. Reports describing the donation state that it included roughly 20,000 Canadian UFO reports, about 10,000 government UFO-related documents, research files, photographs, publications, notes and a substantial specialist library assembled over decades. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://universityaffairs.ca/news/the-university-of-manitobas-archive-of-the-paranormal-just-became-a-little-more-extraordinary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universityaffairs.ca">[University Affairs+2Mental Floss]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universityaffairs.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University AffairsThe University of Manitoba&#x27;s archive of the paranormal just...Feb 25, 2020 — The donation includes 20,000 UFO reports...</span></span></span>

Several features make the collection distinctive:

* **Civilian case files:** reports gathered through decades of private investigation and the [Canadian UFO Survey]({{ 'survey-data/' | relative_url }}).
* **Government records:** documents originating from Canadian agencies that handled UFO reports in different periods.
* **Research notes and correspondence:** material showing how cases were investigated rather than merely reported.
* **Books and publications:** a record of how UFO claims were discussed and interpreted across time.
* **Physical artefacts:** objects associated with notable Manitoba cases, especially [Falcon Lake]({{ 'falcon-lake/' | relative_url }}). <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://universityaffairs.ca/news/the-university-of-manitobas-archive-of-the-paranormal-just-became-a-little-more-extraordinary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universityaffairs.ca">[University Affairs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universityaffairs.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University AffairsThe University of Manitoba&#x27;s archive of the paranormal just...Feb 25, 2020 — The donation includes 20,000 UFO reports...</span></span></span>

For historians, sociologists and sceptical investigators alike, these records create a paper trail. A witness claim can be compared with police documents, media coverage, later interviews and government correspondence. That is often more valuable than the claim itself. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/ufo-reports-donated-to-canadian-university" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: syfy.com">[SYFY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">syfy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo reports donated to canadian university</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thousands of official government UFO reports just...Dec 18, 2019 — Canadian ufologist Chris Rutkowski donates thousands of UFO repor...</span></span></span>

## How Archives Change Private Claims into Inspectable Records

A UFO report is often difficult to evaluate when it exists only as a personal story. An archive changes that by preserving context.

Consider what happens when a case enters an archival collection:

1. Original reports can be compared with later retellings.
2. Dates, locations and witness statements can be checked against official records.
3. Researchers can identify inconsistencies or corroboration.
4. Physical items can be documented and re-examined.
5. Future investigators are not dependent on a single researcher’s interpretation.

This process does not automatically validate a UFO claim. In fact, it frequently exposes weaknesses. A case that sounds persuasive in a documentary may look less convincing when original documents reveal contradictions, missing evidence or alternative explanations. Equally, a case dismissed in popular discussion may appear stronger when contemporary records show multiple witnesses or official attention. The value of the archive lies in making those assessments possible. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://universityaffairs.ca/news/the-university-of-manitobas-archive-of-the-paranormal-just-became-a-little-more-extraordinary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universityaffairs.ca">[University Affairs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universityaffairs.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University AffairsThe University of Manitoba&#x27;s archive of the paranormal just...Feb 25, 2020 — The donation includes 20,000 UFO reports...</span></span></span>

The University of Manitoba archive therefore functions less as a museum of mysteries and more as a research infrastructure. It allows questions to remain open while preserving the evidence needed to examine them. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eventscalendar.umanitoba.ca/site/libraries/event/the-falcon-lake-ufo-files/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eventscalendar.umanitoba.ca">[Events Calendar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eventscalendar.umanitoba.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Events Calendar Libraries</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Events CalendarLibraries - “The Falcon Lake UFO Files”Nov 7, 2019 — With this lecture, Chris Rutkowski launches the creation of his UFO A...</span></span></span>

## What the Falcon Lake Artefacts Can and Cannot Prove

The most famous materials in the collection relate to the 1967 Falcon Lake incident, often described as Canada's best-known UFO case. The archive includes documents and artefacts associated with Stefan Michalak’s account, including burned clothing and investigative records connected to the event. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://universityaffairs.ca/news/the-university-of-manitobas-archive-of-the-paranormal-just-became-a-little-more-extraordinary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universityaffairs.ca">[University Affairs+2SYFY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universityaffairs.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University AffairsThe University of Manitoba&#x27;s archive of the paranormal just...Feb 25, 2020 — The donation includes 20,000 UFO reports...</span></span></span>

These artefacts are important because they are tangible. Unlike many UFO stories, Falcon Lake produced physical items, medical records, police involvement and extensive documentation. Supporters of the case argue that this combination makes it unusually difficult to dismiss outright. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/en/article/this-university-just-acquired-a-massive-collection-of-ufo-files/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">this university just acquired a massive collection of ufo files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This University Just Acquired a Massive Collection of UFO...Dec 10, 2019 — Rutkowski believes that the Falcon Lake case is one of th...</span></span></span>

However, preserving an artefact is not the same as proving what caused it.

The burned shirt can demonstrate that a shirt exists and that it was damaged. It cannot, by itself, demonstrate that a non-human craft caused the damage. Similarly, documents showing investigations occurred demonstrate that authorities took the report seriously enough to examine it; they do not establish that investigators identified an extraordinary object. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/ufo-reports-donated-to-canadian-university" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: syfy.com">[SYFY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">syfy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo reports donated to canadian university</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thousands of official government UFO reports just...Dec 18, 2019 — Canadian ufologist Chris Rutkowski donates thousands of UFO repor...</span></span></span>

The Falcon Lake record also illustrates why archives matter. Over the decades, supporters and critics have reached very different conclusions from much of the same evidence. Some researchers view the case as unusually strong because of witness testimony, medical attention and physical traces. Others point to disputed details, questions about alleged radioactive debris, changing accounts and alternative explanations.
