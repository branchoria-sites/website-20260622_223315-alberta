## Introduction

Anyone looking for [Nova Scotia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-nova-scotias-ufo/' | relative_url }}) UFO records quickly discovers that there is no single Canadian “UFO archive”. Instead, the surviving [paper trail]({{ 'paper-trail/' | relative_url }}) is spread across federal departments, military files, police records, transport reports and scientific collections. That matters because many of the province’s best-known sightings are known today only because they passed through those bureaucratic systems and were preserved. At the same time, many smaller Nova Scotia reports remain difficult to verify because they generated little paperwork or because only fragments of the original records survive. The result is a historical record that is valuable but uneven: rich for a few major incidents, thin for many ordinary sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown2 Mar 2026 — These documents were accumulated between 1947 and the early 1980s and represe...</span></span></span>

## Which Canadian agencies appear in UFO records?

The federal UFO record developed gradually after the late 1940s. Responsibility moved between several agencies over the decades, creating a patchwork archive rather than a single investigation programme. For Nova Scotia cases, readers most commonly encounter records from:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Department of National Defence (DND)**, including Royal Canadian Air Force reporting channels.
* **Department of Transport**, which received aviation-related reports and information from civil aviation personnel.
* **Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP)**, whose detachments often received witness reports first.
* **National Research Council (NRC)**, particularly scientific collections dealing with unusual aerial observations.
* **Canadian Coast Guard and search-and-rescue authorities**, especially in maritime cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada+2batadora.trentu.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown2 Mar 2026 — These documents were accumulated between 1947 and the early 1980s and represe...</span></span></span>

Library and Archives Canada has digitised thousands of federal UFO-related documents, including correspondence, reports, memoranda and reporting procedures accumulated between 1947 and the early 1980s. These files were created for administrative reasons rather than to prove or disprove UFO claims. As a result, they often contain witness descriptions, internal discussions and investigative steps rather than definitive conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown2 Mar 2026 — These documents were accumulated between 1947 and the early 1980s and represe...</span></span></span>

## Why dates, places and metadata can mislead

A common mistake is to treat an archival entry as a complete historical record. Library and Archives Canada itself warns that UFO databases and archival descriptions can contain inconsistencies in dates, locations and indexing because the material originated in multiple departments using different filing systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown2 Mar 2026 — These documents were accumulated between 1947 and the early 1980s and represe...</span></span></span>

This creates several problems for Nova Scotia researchers.

First, a sighting may appear under the date a report was filed rather than the date it occurred. Second, a location may reflect an administrative office rather than the place where witnesses actually observed the event. Third, some records survive only as summaries, while supporting correspondence may be scattered elsewhere in the archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown2 Mar 2026 — These documents were accumulated between 1947 and the early 1980s and represe...</span></span></span>

The practical consequence is that searches for a particular town or year can miss relevant material. A researcher looking for a coastal Nova Scotia case might need to search multiple spellings, nearby communities or broader regional terms before finding all associated records. This is one reason why apparently simple questions—such as how many UFO sightings occurred in a particular Nova Scotia county—often produce uncertain answers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown2 Mar 2026 — These documents were accumulated between 1947 and the early 1980s and represe...</span></span></span>

Another complication is survivorship bias. Cases that involved police, military personnel, pilots or search-and-rescue authorities were more likely to generate documentation. Reports made only to a local newspaper, neighbour or private UFO investigator may leave little trace in federal collections. The archive therefore reflects reporting systems as much as it reflects the underlying phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://batadora.trentu.ca/objects/etd-776" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: batadora.trentu.ca">[batadora.trentu.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">batadora.trentu.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">etd 776</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A History of Canada&#x27;s UFO Investigation, 1950-1995From 1950-1995, the Canadian government investigated the phenomenon of unidentified fly...</span></span></span>

## Why Shag Harbour stands apart

The famous 1967 [Shag Harbour]({{ 'shag-harbour/' | relative_url }}) incident illustrates what a well-documented Nova Scotia case looks like in archival terms.

Witnesses reported lights descending toward the water near Shag Harbour on 4 October 1967. Local authorities initially treated the event as a possible aircraft crash. RCMP officers responded, search-and-rescue authorities were alerted, vessels searched the area and military channels became involved. Because multiple agencies generated paperwork, the incident produced an unusually extensive documentary trail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://recherche-research.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/public/list/43130" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: recherche-research.bac-lac.gc.ca">[LAC Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">recherche-research.bac-lac.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LAC Research1967 Shag Harbour UFO Sighting and Related Research26 Sept 2024 — The Shag Harbour UFO sighting on 4 October 1967 is Canada&#x27;s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 1967</span></span></span>

Library and Archives Canada now maintains a dedicated research collection for Shag Harbour-related material and describes the event as Canada's most famous UFO incident. The surviving records include correspondence, reports and later historical material connected to the case and its legacy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://recherche-research.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/public/list/43130" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: recherche-research.bac-lac.gc.ca">[LAC Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">recherche-research.bac-lac.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LAC Research1967 Shag Harbour UFO Sighting and Related Research26 Sept 2024 — The Shag Harbour UFO sighting on 4 October 1967 is Canada&#x27;s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 1967</span></span></span>

What makes Shag Harbour valuable to historians is not that it proves an extraordinary explanation. Rather, it demonstrates how a sighting entered official systems.
