## Introduction

Many [Prince Edward Island]({{ 'prince-edward-island/' | relative_url }}) UFO reports begin with a simple observation: a bright light apparently hovering above the water. In a surprising number of cases, the mystery is not an exotic object in the sky but a harbour, vessel, buoy, range light or lighthouse whose true position is hidden by darkness, distance or atmospheric conditions. Along PEI’s coasts, dark water often removes the visual clues that normally allow people to judge height and range. A light that is actually near the horizon can appear suspended in mid-air, while a vessel travelling directly towards or away from an observer may seem motionless for long periods. This mechanism is one of the most important misidentification risks in the province’s UFO history because many reports involve distant lights seen across bays, harbours and coastal channels rather than close encounters with structured objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2024/mpo-dfo/Fs154-52-1-2023-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.gc.ca">[Publications.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The distance to a light cannot be reliably estimated...</span></span></span>

## Why lights over dark water lose context

The essential problem is that water provides very few reference points at night. Trees, buildings, roads and hills help people estimate distance and altitude on land. Looking across a dark harbour or bay, those cues often disappear.

A bright light viewed across open water can therefore seem detached from its source. If the observer cannot see the shoreline beneath it, the light may appear to float above the horizon. Slight haze, sea spray or low cloud can further blur the boundary between sky and water, making a fixed light seem airborne. The effect becomes stronger when only a single bright point is visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2024/mpo-dfo/Fs154-52-1-2023-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.gc.ca">[Publications.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The distance to a light cannot be reliably estimated...</span></span></span>

Canadian navigation guidance specifically warns that atmospheric conditions can dramatically affect the visibility of lights and that distance cannot be reliably estimated from apparent brightness. A very bright harbour or navigation light may therefore look much closer than it really is, encouraging [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }}) to interpret it as a nearby hovering object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2024/mpo-dfo/Fs154-52-1-2023-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.gc.ca">[Publications.gc.ca+2Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The distance to a light cannot be reliably estimated...</span></span></span>

Within PEI UFO reporting, this matters because many sightings originate from beaches, wharves and coastal roads where witnesses are looking across substantial stretches of dark water. The original report may accurately describe what was seen while still misidentifying what produced it.

## Boats, buoys and shore signals in PEI reports

Prince Edward Island's coastline contains numerous legitimate light sources that can be mistaken for aerial phenomena when viewed from the wrong angle.

Harbour approaches are marked by navigation aids with carefully designed light patterns. These include lighthouses, range lights, harbour entrance lights and illuminated buoys. Many are intended to be visible over considerable distances and can appear isolated against a dark horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/library-bibliotheque/4105913x.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca">[Waves Vagues+2RCMSAR 12]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">light above water level, in metres may be</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Waves VaguesQuick Reference Guide to Canada&#x27;s Navigational Aids (...August 21, 2023 — Major lighted aids to navigation may display a mai...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2023</span></span></span>

Examples around PEI include historic and active harbour lights such as the North Rustico Harbour Light, St. Peters Harbour Light and various range-light systems used to guide vessels into channels and harbours. These installations are designed specifically to attract attention and remain visible in poor conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peters_Harbour_Light" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+3Wikipedia+3Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">St. Peters Harbour Light</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">St. Peters Harbour Light</span></span></span>

* **A vessel travelling towards the observer** may show little apparent sideways motion, creating the illusion of a stationary hovering light.

* **Range lights aligned from a particular viewpoint** can resemble a single unusually bright object.
* **Lights partly obscured by haze** may appear to brighten, dim or disappear suddenly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/library-bibliotheque/4105913x.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca">[Waves Vagues+2RCMSAR 12]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">light above water level, in metres may be</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Waves VaguesQuick Reference Guide to Canada&#x27;s Navigational Aids (...August 21, 2023 — Major lighted aids to navigation may display a mai...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why some harbour lights seem to hover

The most distinctive feature of these reports is the claim that the light remained motionless.

A hovering appearance often emerges because observers unconsciously expect a distant object to show sideways movement. Yet a vessel moving directly towards shore, or a fixed navigation light seen from many kilometres away, may show almost none. With no visible waterline and no obvious landmarks nearby, the brain interprets the bright point as suspended in space.

Another factor is the intensity of marine navigation lighting. Canadian navigation publications note that major aids can be visible for many nautical miles and may have distinctive flash patterns or emergency lighting systems. Seen from a distance, these characteristics can look deliberate or unusual to someone unfamiliar with marine navigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rcmsar12.org/page/aids" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rcmsar12.org">[RCMSAR 12+2Waves Vagues]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rcmsar12.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RCMSAR 12Aids to Navigation21 May 2026 — Major lighted aids to navigation may display a main light, are equipped with an emergency light...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

Witnesses sometimes report that a light remained fixed for several minutes before suddenly vanishing. In coastal environments this can occur when a vessel changes heading, passes behind shoreline features, enters fog, or moves beyond the visible arc of a navigation light. A disappearance that feels abrupt from one viewing position may be entirely normal from another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/canadian-coast-guard/corporate/publications/canadian-aids-navigation-system-2023.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">canadian aids navigation system 2023</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Canadian Aids to Navigation System 202314 Apr 2026 — Atmospheric conditions can have a considerable effect on light transmissio...</span></span></span>

## What details make a harbour-light case testable

Harbour-light [explanations]({{ 'explanations-b5b0c8/' | relative_url }}) are strongest when investigators can compare a report with known navigation aids and marine traffic. Fortunately, this is one of the more testable categories of PEI UFO sighting.

Several details are particularly valuable:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Exact observation location.
* Direction of the light relative to the shoreline.
* Time and duration.
* Weather and visibility conditions.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2024/mpo-dfo/Fs154-52-1-2023-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.gc.ca">[publications.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The distance to a light cannot be reliably estimated...</span></span></span>
* Whether the light flashed, pulsed or changed colour.
* Apparent movement relative to known landmarks.
* Information about vessel traffic in the area.

</div>

These details can often be checked against published lists of lights, harbour approaches, navigation charts and known lighthouse characteristics. Canada maintains extensive documentation on marine aids to navigation, including light colours, flash sequences, heights and nominal ranges.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/library-bibliotheque/4105913x.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca">[Waves Vagues+2Publications.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">light above water level, in metres may be</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Waves VaguesQuick Reference Guide to Canada&#x27;s Navigational Aids (...August 21, 2023 — Major lighted aids to navigation may display a mai...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2023</span></span></span>

A report that merely states "a bright light hovered over the water" remains difficult to assess. A report that identifies the viewing point, compass direction and behaviour of the light can often be investigated much more rigorously.

## Why this mechanism matters in PEI UFO history

Harbour-light misidentifications are not among the most dramatic explanations for UFO reports, but they are among the most important. They demonstrate how sincere observations can become puzzling when viewed in a coastal environment that strips away normal depth and distance cues.

For Prince Edward Island, where long shoreline vistas and maritime activity are central features of the landscape, bright harbour lights provide a recurring reminder that the first challenge in any UFO investigation is determining whether a light was truly in the sky at all. Before asking what an object might have been, investigators must establish whether the apparent object was actually a distant vessel, buoy, range light or harbour beacon whose connection to the water had simply disappeared into the darkness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2024/mpo-dfo/Fs154-52-1-2023-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.gc.ca">[Publications.gc.ca+2Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The distance to a light cannot be reliably estimated...</span></span></span>
