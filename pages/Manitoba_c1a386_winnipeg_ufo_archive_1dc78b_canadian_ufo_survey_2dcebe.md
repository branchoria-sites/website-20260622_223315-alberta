## Introduction

The Canadian UFO Survey is one of the most useful tools for understanding UFO reporting in Canada, but it is often misunderstood. For more than three decades, researchers led by Winnipeg-based investigator Chris Rutkowski and Ufology Research have collected reports from civilian organisations, government sources, investigators and witnesses across the country. The result is a long-running national database that helps identify [patterns]({{ 'patterns/' | relative_url }}) in what Canadians report seeing in the sky. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://canadianuforeport.ca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadianuforeport.ca">[Canadian UFO Report+2Canadian UFO Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadianuforeport.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canadian UFO ReportCanadian UFO SurveyReport a Sighting! What the survey does **not** do is prove that unexplained sightings are alien spacecraft. Its value lies elsewhere. It shows how often reports occur, where they cluster, what kinds of objects people describe, how many cases receive ordinary explanations, and how many remain unresolved because the available information is incomplete or inconclusive. Understanding that distinction is essential when using the survey as evidence in [Manitoba]({{ 'why-manitoba-matters-in-canadian-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s UFO history and in the broader story of Winnipeg's role in preserving Canada's UFO records. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://themanitoban.com/2014/03/keep-lookin-up/19590/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: themanitoban.com">[the Manitoban+2Science.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">themanitoban.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">keep lookin up</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">the Manitoban&#x27;Keep lookin&#x27; up&#x27; &#124; the Manitoban17 Mar 2014 — “UFOs exist. ## How the survey collects and categorises reports

The Canadian UFO Survey began in 1989 as an effort to gather reports from multiple reporting channels into a single annual dataset. Rather than relying on one organisation, it compiles sightings from investigators, civilian reporting groups, government contacts and other sources. This approach was particularly important in Canada because UFO reports historically moved through a fragmented mix of federal departments, aviation authorities, police agencies and private researchers rather than through a single national office. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://canadianuforeport.ca/our-team" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadianuforeport.ca">[Canadian UFO Report+2Science.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadianuforeport.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Canadian UFO Report Our Team at Canadian UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manitoba), or UFOROM, has solicited sightings data from active Canadian researchers. ## What unexplained cases do and do not mean

One of the most common misunderstandings about the Canadian UFO Survey is the assumption that unresolved reports are evidence of non-human technology. The survey itself does not support that conclusion.

The Office of the Chief Science Advisor's Sky Canada project noted that many reports remain unresolved because witness descriptions are incomplete, supporting evidence is lacking, or the available information is too vague for a reliable identification. Similar patterns appear in civilian UFO databases where a significant share of reports cannot be conclusively explained, often because investigators do not have enough data rather than because a mystery object has been confirmed. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.gc.ca">[Science.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">it investigated in 2023, 31% could not be conclusively explained due to...</span></span></span>

A useful way to think about the survey is that it measures three different things at once:

**Reporting behaviour.** It shows what Canadians choose to report and when they are motivated to report it.

**Identification success.** It shows how often ordinary explanations can be matched to reported events.

**Residual uncertainty.** It reveals the number of cases that remain unresolved after available information has been reviewed. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.gc.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/sky-canada-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.gc.ca">[Science.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Management of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial...28 Jun 2025 — During the Sky Canada Project, the Office of the Chief Science...</span></span></span>

Residual uncertainty is not proof of anything extraordinary. In many scientific fields, unresolved observations simply indicate that the evidence is incomplete. The survey's unexplained category therefore represents unanswered questions, not verified alien craft.

## Why long-term data matter more than individual headlines

Individual UFO stories often attract public attention because they are dramatic. The survey's strength is that it shifts attention from isolated anecdotes to long-term trends.

A single report may be influenced by weather conditions, media coverage, witness expectations or local circumstances. A database spanning decades allows researchers to ask different questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Do reports increase during major media cycles?
* Are certain types of sightings becoming more common?
* Do reporting rates change with new technologies such as drones?
* Are there seasonal patterns?
* Which provinces generate the largest numbers of reports? <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://canadianuforeport.ca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadianuforeport.ca">[Canadian UFO Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadianuforeport.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canadian UFO ReportCanadian UFO SurveyReport a Sighting! This broader perspective is one reason the Sky Canada project described the Canadian UFO Survey as Canada's longest-running and most recognised collection of UFO sighting data. The value lies not in any single case but in the ability to compare thousands of reports over time. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.gc.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/sky-canada-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.gc.ca">[Science.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Management of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial...28 Jun 2025 — During the Sky Canada Project, the Office of the Chief Science...</span></span></span>

The dataset also helps reveal how new technologies affect UFO reporting. Satellites, drone activity, Starlink launches and widespread smartphone use have all changed the kinds of observations people make and the evidence they submit. ## How Manitoba fits into the national pattern

Manitoba's importance goes beyond its own sightings. The province became one of the key centres of Canadian UFO documentation because the Canadian UFO Survey was coordinated from Winnipeg for decades. As a result, Manitoba occupies a unique place in the national record trail.

The province is home to famous cases such as [Falcon Lake]({{ 'falcon-lake/' | relative_url }}), but its larger historical significance is archival. Winnipeg became a hub where reports from across Canada could be assembled, compared and preserved. That role eventually contributed to the development of major archival collections associated with Canadian UFO research. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.de/books?cad=1&amp;hl=de&amp;id=fq5iP65y3JgC&amp;source=gbs_citations_module_r" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.de">[Google Books+2Science.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksThe Canadian UFO Report: The Best Cases RevealedWas a prospector burned by a UFO in Manitoba in 1967?
